Drug Testing Panels Explained

The 5 panel drug test, specifically the urine test, is one of the most common drug tests and is the drug test of choice for the Department of Transportation (DOT).

5 Panel

The drugs tested in a 5 panel drug test are as follows:

5 Panel Drug Test

  • Amphetamines, including methamphetamines
  • Cocaine
  • Marijuana
  • Opiates
  • Phencyclidine (PCP)

Plus Expanded Opiates

Expanded opiates cover many prescription drugs used for moderate to severe pain and can be added to a 5 or 10 panel drug test.

  • Hydrocodone
  • Hydromorphone
  • Codeine
  • Morphine
  • Oxycodone
  • Oxymorphone
  • Vicodin, Lortab, Lorcet, and Dilaudid references when applicable
  • Heroin metabolite when included by the ordered panel

10 Panel

A 10 panel drug test includes the drugs tested in a 5 panel and is commonly used when an employer, agency, or individual needs to look for substances beyond the standard 5 panel.

  • Amphetamines
  • Barbiturates
  • Benzodiazepines
  • Cocaine
  • Marijuana
  • MDA
  • Methadone
  • Methaqualone, lab-based only
  • Opiates
  • PCP
  • Propoxyphene

12 Panel

The 12 panel drug test is one of the most comprehensive panel options.

  • Amphetamines
  • Barbiturates

  • Demerol

  • 6-AM Heroin

Collection Methods

The collection methods for drug tests include urine collection, hair follicle sample, and blood draw. The appropriate collection method depends on the requested test, the purpose of testing, and applicable program requirements.

Urine

The procedure for a urine drug test is simple. A donor provides a urine specimen in a cup to a drug testing collection specialist, who seals the specimen cup, completes the required documentation including the chain of custody form, has the donor initial the specimen cup seal, verifies identification, and sends the specimen in a secure manner by courier to the laboratory for testing.

Hair

A hair drug test is a process where a drug testing collection specialist cuts approximately 120 strands of hair from the head, or a specific amount from the body, and submits the hair to a certified laboratory for analysis. If a donor does not have 1.5 inches of hair on the head, body hair may be used. If the donor is bald and has no or very little body hair, a hair drug test may not be available, and a urine or oral/saliva test may be used instead.

Blood

Drug testing using a blood draw is not as common as urine testing, but it is still used by some governmental agencies and court systems. Blood drug testing measures whether a person has an illegal drug metabolite in their system at the time the blood is drawn.
